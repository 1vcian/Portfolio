Open source · Security research

Brucia

A €50 board and the phone in your pocket, turned into a full security-research kit. A LilyGO T⁠-⁠Embed CC1101 handles the radios (Wi⁠-⁠Fi, BLE, Sub⁠-⁠GHz, infrared, NFC) over one USB cable, and the phone you already own brings the compute, the storage, the GPS and the screen. The phone is the brain, the ESP32 is the antenna.

Wi⁠-⁠Fi tests you would not even think of running on a Flipper Zero or on Bruce. Handshakes and PMKIDs captured by the board get their WPA/WPA2 dictionary checks on the phone's CPU. Monitor-mode captures open as PCAPs with packet and protocol detail, and a lab HTTP/HTTPS inspection proxy runs on the phone with a proper interface.

GPS wardriving, with the GPS you already carry. The phone tags every access point with its position, removes duplicates, names the vendor and draws tracks and markers on an offline map, then exports to WiGLE, KML or GPX. On Sub⁠-⁠GHz it plans De Bruijn sweeps, the shortest sequence that contains every fixed code, and sends them to the radio in bursts.

A native Kotlin app on top of Bruce-derived firmware, for authorized testing only. Every release ships a web flasher, a wiki and prebuilt firmware for dozens of boards.

2On the stores

iOS & Android · Word game

Parolix

A vintage-newspaper word puzzle in 17 languages: daily riddles, endless runs, 32 living editions (each with its own animated scene, six of them earned only through streak and score milestones) and one world leaderboard with weekly prizes. Under the hood the backend is built to cost next to nothing: cloud saves are signed with SHA-256 in a 10-slot ring at one read and one write per session, and the leaderboard is static JSON that CI rebuilds every hour, so reading it never touches the database.

It also markets itself. A GitHub Actions pipeline scripts an episode, plays it in headless Chromium on virtual time (frame-perfect 60 fps on a runner with no GPU), edits it with ffmpeg and schedules it on YouTube Shorts at each language's peak hour, rotating eight formats (commented game, guess-it countdown, real-or-fake, anagram, edition showcase, word meaning, endless run, shareable front page). 100+ Shorts published so far, hands-off.

4On the stores

iOS & Android · Party game

Wolf & Mafia

The narrator in your pocket for the party game of wolves, villagers and secret roles (Mafia, Werewolf, Lupus in Tabula), built from scratch: 39 characters, a guided night step by step that the phone can read aloud, and protections, deaths and wins worked out by the app. 100% offline, in 12 languages.

Phones talk to each other through sound, with no network. I designed a compact binary protocol (a hash of the game's identity, idempotent sequence numbers, bit masks, CRC8) that travels on a near-ultrasonic audio modem (ggwave): at dawn the narrator's phone updates every player's phone with no internet, Wi⁠-⁠Fi or Bluetooth. A tablet or TV can show the table, and players without the app see their role in the browser.

Underneath, an event-sourced rules engine: the game is a replay of the narrator's choices, so every step can be undone, and the rules are checked by playing whole games automatically. The role setups it proposes are balanced the same way. No server at all.

Open source · Computer vision

Card Scanner

The idea: the model only finds the cards, it never learns them. An OBB detection model locates each card's shape in the image, and a perceptual hash matches the crop against the card database. A new set only needs its pictures in the database: no retraining. It all runs client-side, in under 10 MB.

It became the core scanner of the popular open-source TCG Pocket Collection Tracker (★ 160+), and it kept travelling: developers of other apps have written to tell me they built the same solution into theirs.

Source-available · Emulation

Vetro

The goal: a complete ARM64 Android system emulated inside a browser tab, with an analysis layer that sees everything apps do from the outside. No install, no root, no server.

It runs Android today. Open the page in Chrome or Edge and the project's own AOSP 15 build with microG reaches its home screen from a prebuilt snapshot; you can install an APK by dragging it onto the page, and an app opens and reacts to touch. Underneath is an AArch64 CPU emulator in Rust compiled to WebAssembly, with a block JIT that emits WASM, checked against QEMU instruction by instruction: Linux 6.18 boots with a log identical to QEMU's and the LTP syscall tests match qemu-aarch64 in CI.

Now in progress: the analysis layer. A network inspector and a timeline that ties each request to the tap that caused it, real internet through a small companion app on your own computer, a live file manager for app data, and record & replay down to a single instruction.

Open source · Capacitor plugin

EGNSS Capacitor

For apps where the position is evidence, not just a blue dot. A phone's location can be several metres off, and it can be spoofed. This plugin gives a Capacitor app one JavaScript API, identical on Android, iOS and Chromium browsers, that says where you are, where the fix came from and how far you can trust it.

From 5 metres to 1 centimetre. On its own a phone is good to about 5 m under open sky. The plugin builds on EGNSS4ALL, the EU's open-source app for geotagged photos in agriculture: it adds Galileo's OSNMA (signals authenticated as really coming from the satellites), EGNOS corrections, which bring a receiver to about 1 m, and EGNSS4ALL's convex-hull averaging to steady the fix. And when that is not enough it reads an external Bluetooth antenna instead: with an RTK receiver such as a u-blox ZED⁠-⁠F9P the same API returns fixes at about 1 cm.

Every fix carries its source, an integrityLevel and the OSNMA status, so the app never branches on the platform. It comes with a demo app: an OpenLayers map on satellite imagery, antenna pairing, and tap to take a geotagged photo.

At work · Satellite imagery

AI Sentinel Image Pipeline

Fresh satellite imagery every month, instead of every three years. A pipeline on Google Cloud Vertex AI selects the best Sentinel⁠-⁠2 scenes each month and upscales them from 10 m to 1 m with a super-resolution model. I found the approach and built the pipeline around it.

The alternative was a 30 cm orthophoto: about €4M, renewed once every three years. Over the same three years the pipeline delivers 36 updates for about €18k (about €500 a month). A 1 m image does not replace 30 cm for fine parcel boundaries, but crops change every season, and a monthly picture shows what is in a field today.
